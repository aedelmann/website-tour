Picture this: we peel out of **Jerez** where the air still feels like someone left the oven on (~30°C of “why is my phone hotter than my optimism”), point the Tesla roughly **600 km north**, spend about **€27** making friends with Superchargers along the way, and roll into **Salamanca** in the afternoon under a sky that has clearly filed a complaint. Cloudy. Windy. A crisp **21°C**. Our summer wardrobe briefly considers mutiny. We did not come for a long weekend—we came for **one night**, because Ferrol and camping in Valdoviño are waiting, and tomorrow’s alarm is set for a deeply unreasonable **7am**.

So the brief was simple: arrive, walk, eat, sleep, leave. Salamanca, bless it, still managed to feel like a proper chapter.

## Golden stone and a city that studies harder than we do

Salamanca is a **university city**—home to the **University of Salamanca**, one of Europe’s oldest—which explains the pleasant density of students, Spanish-language schools, and people who look like they know which century that façade belongs to. The old town is **UNESCO**-listed, the buildings glow that famous **Villamayor sandstone** honey-gold even when the weather refuses to cooperate, and somewhere in the middle of it all sits **Plaza Mayor**, doing the “I am the living room of Spain” thing with quiet confidence. Two cathedrals. Enough ornate stone to make your neck file a formal complaint. We kept it light on the guidebook lecture and heavy on the “wow, look up.”

## Walk first, dinner second, dignity optional

We did what overnight stops do best: **walked the city**, stared at architecture until we ran out of synonyms for “intricate,” found dinner without turning it into a Michelin scavenger hunt, and then retreated to rest like responsible adults who have a 7am departure date with Galicia. No heroic itinerary. No pretending we “did Salamanca.” Just enough wandering to know we would happily come back when the alarm is not already plotting against us.

The wind kept us honest. The sandstone kept us grinning. The pink-and-white flower wall under the big metal **SALAMANCA** letters did the postcard job so well we almost forgave the clouds.

## Key takeaways

1. **Not very touristy**—in the best way: real town energy, not a theme-park queue with better fonts.
2. **Nice restaurant and pub scene**—easy to land a good dinner after a long drive without turning the evening into a research project.
3. **Windy and chilly (21°C)**—especially funny when your brain is still calibrated to Jerez heat.
4. **Old but nice**, with **wonderful architecture**—golden stone, cathedrals, convents, and shell-studded façades that make “just one more street” a dangerous sentence.

We left Salamanca with colder fingers, warmer opinions, and a Tesla that had already earned its keep on the Jerez-to-north run. Next stop: Ferrol direction, camping Valdoviño, and whatever 7am looks like when you still smell faintly of sandstone dust and residual Andalusian summer.

*Hasta luego, Salamanca—you had us at golden stone and kept us with “wait, it’s only 21 degrees?”*
